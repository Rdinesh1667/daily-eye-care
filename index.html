How to follow the routine

1. Set up gently
  • Sit comfortably with your head relaxed and shoulders down.
  • Use comfortable lighting; do not stare into bright light.
  • These practices may help relaxation or screen comfort, but they are not proven to reverse refractive errors or remove the need for glasses.
2. Six-point orbital-bone massage
  1. Wash your hands first.
  2. With a fingertip, use very light circular touches along the bony rim above the eye, avoiding the eyelid margin.
  3. Continue gently around the outer and lower bony rim if comfortable.
  4. Do not push on the eyeball, rub hard, or massage an injured or painful eye.

If you have had recent eye surgery or an eye injury, skip massage unless your clinician says it is safe.

3. Gentle eye movements
  • Keep your head still and move your gaze up and down, then left and right, only through a comfortable range.
  • If comfortable, glance diagonally in each direction and make slow, small circles.
  • Try a few natural blinks; avoid squeezing or holding your eyes open.
  • Optional near–far focus: slowly alternate between a nearby object and a distant object, without forcing focus.

Keep this brief and easy. Stop if you experience pain, headache, dizziness, double vision or worsening symptoms.

4. Outdoor daylight
  • Spend time outside during daylight when feasible. A walk or sitting in a shaded outdoor area is fine.
  • Use normal sun protection as appropriate, including a hat or UV-protective sunglasses.
  • Never look directly at the sun, even at sunrise or sunset.
5. Rest / palming alternative
  • Warm your hands by rubbing them together, then close your eyes gently.
  • Hover your cupped palms in front of the eyes or rest them lightly on the surrounding bones without pressure.
  • Breathe calmly for 30–60 seconds. Keep pressure off the eyeballs.
6. Screen and evening habits
  • Use the 20-20-20 reminder: about every 20 minutes, look roughly 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
  • Increase text size if needed, reduce glare and keep the screen at a comfortable distance.
  • Make a conscious effort to blink regularly during screen use.
  • Prioritize sleep and take breaks when eyes feel tired.
7. Glasses and vision checks
  • Continue wearing prescribed glasses or contact lenses as directed.
  • Do not reduce a prescription based on this routine or an informal home test.
  • Arrange an eye examination for persistent blur, eye strain or changing vision.
Safety first: seek urgent medical care for sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, new flashes or many new floaters, or a curtain/shadow over your vision. Stop any activity that causes pain or worsens symptoms.

Suggested 21-day rhythm

  • Days 1–7: learn the steps; keep everything gentle and short.
  • Days 8–14: focus on consistency, outdoor time and regular screen breaks.
  • Days 15–21: continue only the habits that feel comfortable and sustainable; review your notes.

This is a wellness and eye-comfort tracker, not a treatment plan. It cannot guarantee improved eyesight or a changed prescription.

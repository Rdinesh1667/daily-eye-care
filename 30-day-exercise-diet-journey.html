    Intensity rule: On moderate cardio, breathe faster but still be able to speak in sentences. Reduce the duration or effort if you cannot recover comfortably.
    Why this plan is designed this way · research & sources

    This is a general starting framework, not a personalized medical plan. The checklist focuses on repeatable actions, gradual progression, a variety of foods, and reflection rather than calorie limits or daily punishment.

    • World Health Organization — Physical activity: adults are advised to build toward 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week and do muscle-strengthening activity on at least 2 days; some activity is better than none.
    • WHO — Start with small amounts and build gradually: increase duration, frequency, and intensity over time, according to ability and health status.
    • ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition — Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024): encourages dietary variety, vegetables and fruits, pulses/other protein sources, and whole grains/millets as part of a balanced pattern.
    • American Heart Association scientific statement — lifestyle behavior change: self-monitoring, goal setting, and feedback can help people recognize progress and barriers.
    Safety before streaks: Adjust this plan to your current fitness, age, health, and access to equipment. If you have a medical condition, a recent injury, are pregnant/postpartum, or are unsure what is safe for you, ask a qualified healthcare professional about adaptations. Stop exercising and seek appropriate medical help for chest pain, fainting, or severe/unusual breathlessness. Rest is part of training—not a failure.
